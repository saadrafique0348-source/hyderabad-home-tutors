How Parents Can Track Their Child’s Progress with a Home Tutor

Paying for a home tutor is only half the job. The other half is knowing whether the lessons are actually helping your child. Without a simple way to check progress, months can pass before a parent realises nothing has changed. Here is how to track your child’s progress in a calm, practical way.

1. Agree clear goals at the start

Before the first lesson, sit with the tutor and your child and agree on one or two clear goals. For example: finish the Class 9 Mathematics syllabus before the exams, or improve English writing. Clear goals give everyone something to measure against. Avoid vague goals like “get better at studies” — they are hard to check.

2. Ask for a short update after each lesson

After each lesson, ask the tutor for a one-minute update: what was covered today, what your child found hard, and what to practise before the next lesson. A good tutor will do this without being asked. If the tutor cannot say what was covered, that is a warning sign. 3. Look at homework and school tests

The simplest check is your child’s own schoolwork. Is homework getting finished on time? Are school test marks moving in the right direction? You do not need to check every answer — just notice the trend over a few weeks. If homework is still a struggle after a month of regular lessons, talk to the tutor about what needs to change.

4. Talk to your child

Ask your child directly: do you understand the tutor? Do you feel more confident in this subject? A child who is learning well usually says so. If your child dreads lessons, cannot explain what they learned, or feels the tutor rushes, take it seriously and discuss it with the tutor early.

5. Have a short monthly check-in

Once a month, have a short conversation with the tutor about progress: what has improved, what is still weak, and what the plan is for the next month. Write the goals down so you can compare month to month. This is also a good time to adjust the number of lessons or the timing if needed.

6. Know when to change something

Consider a change if, after a few weeks, homework is no easier, your child still cannot explain the topic, the tutor gives no updates, or lessons are often late or cancelled. Talk to the tutor first — a good tutor will adjust. If nothing changes, it is reasonable to look for a different tutor. Tracking progress does not need tests or spreadsheets. Clear goals, a short update after each lesson, a look at schoolwork, and a monthly chat with the tutor are enough. Stay involved, and the tuition will do far more for your child.
